About

I'm a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at NYU Tandon, advised by Dr. Katharina Schultebraucks at the NYU Grossman School of Medicine, Department of Psychiatry. My research sits in computational psychiatry: I design human-subjects studies and build models that turn multimodal behavioral and physiological time series into signals of stress, distress and mental-health risk.

Before NYU, I studied Medical Engineering at FAU Erlangen-Nuremberg (B.Sc. and M.Sc.), spent an Erasmus+ semester at the Technion in Haifa, and worked on SPECT imaging research at Siemens Healthineers. This semester I'm also taking a course on robotics in healthcare, from catheter robots to sensors, motors and control.

Outside the lab I play competitive tennis (former team captain and coach), and I'm interested in philosophy, effective philanthropy and meditation.

Research

LLMs for PTSD risk prediction

PREVIA, a multi-LLM framework that predicts early PTSD risk from patients' spoken trauma narratives in the emergency department.

Detecting acute distress in video

Multimodal behavioral time series, anomaly detection and generative AI to find moments of acute emotional distress in real-world ED recordings.

Physiology meets behavior

ECG, EDA and respiration recording in two NIH-funded studies, linking physiological stress responses to face, voice, movement and gaze.

Passive sensing & suicide risk

Modeling suicidal ideation from passive-sensing time-series data in a post-psychiatric-discharge cohort, with clinical partners.

Currently: Robotics

This semester I'm taking BE-GY 6353, Intelligent Healthcare Systems and Technologies, a course on robots for cardiac and neurovascular catheter procedures. Selected coursework:

01

IMU Orientation Lab

Connected an IM948 IMU over USB and measured roll, pitch and yaw with the accelerometer and gyroscope.

Identified gimbal lock near 90° pitch and explained why heading needs a magnetometer.

02

Cardiac Catheter Procedures

Compared PCI, TAVR, TMVR and AF ablation by clinical target, access route and device.

Named the main technical challenge of each and the degrees of freedom a robot must control.

03

Neurovascular Robot Actuation

Calculated roller speeds, gear ratios and DC motor torque for a catheter-driving robot.

Set a 30 mA current limit to keep catheter force at or below 0.30 N, and explained its limits.
